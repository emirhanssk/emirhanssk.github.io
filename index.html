01 / RELEASED ON ANDROID

Fallune!

A mobile arcade sorting game built around directional swipes, accuracy and timing.

UnityC#Mobile gameplay

The player loop

A box travels toward a decision point. The player reads its direction and swipes at the right moment. Each result feeds the score, combo and level progress, making a simple gesture the centre of the challenge.

Mechanics & implementation

  • A decision window and a tighter perfect-input zone reward precise timing.
  • Campaign objectives and an endless mode support different play sessions.
  • Combo tracking, audio and haptic feedback reinforce successful inputs.
Under the hood +

Gameplay, input, UI and service responsibilities are separated into dedicated systems. The project includes local saves and cloud synchronization, achievements, leaderboard UI and analytics integration. Box prefabs and materials are cached; active boxes and expected directions are tracked in pre-sized collections.

Technical description reflects the reviewed repository. Store availability may vary by region.

02 / MOVEMENT PROTOTYPE

Battle Orders

A regiment-based RTS project exploring how soldiers move as a coherent formation.

Unity / URPC# simulationFormation geometry

The design problem

A regiment should feel organised without moving like one rigid block. Ranks follow the same route and enter turns in sequence. Within each rank, soldiers keep their lateral spacing while the inner wing slows and the outer wing travels farther.

Mechanics & implementation

  • Long orders use rank-following movement; nearby orders use individual paths to reform.
  • Arc–straight–arc routes are smoothed, then checked against curvature limits.
  • Obstacle clearance, minimum spacing and neighbouring regiments constrain safe movement.
  • Arrival position and facing are part of the order; drag gestures define the target frontage.
Geometry & performance +

For formation turns, the route radius must exceed the formation's half-width. This keeps the inner wing moving forward rather than requiring backward steps. Rank spacing and stretch limits prevent the formation from separating excessively.

The simulation uses planar C# geometry without UnityEngine dependencies. The 3D presentation reads the route data used by the movement system. Headless tools measure order time and allocation, verify route parity and exercise movement scenarios.

The benchmark harness includes three 24 × 6 regiments (432 soldiers). Screenshot FPS is a development capture, not a hardware-independent performance claim. Multiplayer and ranked play remain project goals.

03 / IN DEVELOPMENT

Cities Tycoon

A tycoon and city-building hybrid where spatial growth and production chains shape each other.

UnityEconomy simulationMobile rendering

The player loop

Build roads, zone housing and grow the population. Expansion increases demand for goods and services. Establish local production, manage imports and improve city services to support the next stage of growth.

Connected mechanics

  • Production chains turn raw resources into intermediate and finished goods.
  • Electricity, water, sewage and garbage capacity interact with the growing city.
  • Education gates advanced products; happiness influences growth and income.
  • Population milestones unlock services and products. Development goals guide the player without relocking progress when population falls.
Built for scale +

Repeated road and residential meshes use GPU instancing. A chunk-based visibility system limits rendering work, while cached transform matrices and reused buffers reduce repeated CPU work.

The economy runs on a configurable tick rather than recomputing every system each rendered frame. Catch-up work is capped at two ticks per frame. Telemetry records city metrics, making demand, capacity and cash-flow behaviour inspectable during balancing.

Profiler and telemetry images show development snapshots. They are not a controlled before-and-after benchmark.

02 / ENGINEERING APPROACH

Make the behaviour clear.
Then make the cost visible.

Good mechanics need
good foundations.

01

Separate the simulation

Keep movement and economy rules distinct from presentation. Inspect the model without relying on the scene to explain it.

02

Measure the right work

Use Unity Profiler, gameplay telemetry and headless timing tools to distinguish rendering cost, simulation behaviour and order processing.

03

Spend work deliberately

Batch repeated geometry, cull invisible chunks, cache reusable data and schedule economy updates around a defined tick budget.
